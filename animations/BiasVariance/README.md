# The Bias–Variance Tradeoff

A short (~3-minute), house-style explainer that fits the **same noisy data** with
three models and lets the numbers tell the story — no voice-over, everything is
on screen. Boxes, points and small graphs; no equations, so it renders without
LaTeX.

![the U-curve: bias² down, variance up, total error a U with a sweet spot](media/preview.png)

## The idea

The data is generated by a hidden **downward parabola** plus random noise, so a
quadratic is genuinely the right model. Every number shown — the polynomial
fits, the MSE / R² values, and the final bias/variance split — is really
computed (a 400-run Monte-Carlo for the decomposition), not faked.

## The film (`BiasVariance`)

Bookended by the channel's intro card and the "Thank you for watching!" outro,
five scenes:

1. **Setup** — 11 noisy observations, a hidden true pattern, and the scoring rule:
   **MSE = mean of (observed − predicted)²**. Lower MSE = closer to the data.
2. **Too simple → underfitting** — a straight line (degree 1). It can't bend to
   the curve, the residuals are big, **MSE = 0.113**, R² = 0.40. *High bias.*
3. **Just right → a good fit** — a quadratic (degree 2). It tracks the trend and
   recovers the hidden pattern, **MSE = 0.022**, R² = 0.88. *Low bias, low variance.*
4. **Too complex → overfitting** — a degree-10 polynomial wiggles through every
   point: **train MSE = 0.000**, R² = 1.00 — looks perfect. Then new, unseen data
   arrives and it's way off: **test MSE = 0.109** (the quadratic scored 0.013).
   *High variance — it memorised the noise.*
5. **The tradeoff** — the U-curve of total error vs. model complexity, with the
   three regimes marked and the **sweet spot** at the bottom; the decomposition
   **total error = bias² + variance** shown as two competing curves.

A "simpler ↔ more complex" balance beam tilts with each model to reinforce the
metaphor: it dips to *simpler* when underfitting, sits level when just right, and
dips to *more complex* when overfitting.

## Rendering

```bash
./render.sh underfit --quick   # fast sanity check of one scene
./render.sh                    # the whole film, 480p
./render.sh full -q m          # final 720p
./render.sh full -q h          # 1080p
./render.sh --stitch -q m      # render each scene and join into one film
```

`render.sh` reuses the HarnessEngineering / CNN / Fourier series' `.venv` if it
finds one (so Manim isn't reinstalled), otherwise it bootstraps a local `.venv`
from `requirements.txt`.

Individually renderable scenes: `intro` · `setup` · `underfit` · `justright` ·
`overfit` · `tradeoff` · `outro` (or `full`, the default).

`BV_QUICK=1` (or `--quick`) shortens the on-screen holds while iterating. The
data, fits, decomposition and all tunables (palette, pacing `DELAY`) live at the
top of `bias_variance.py`.
